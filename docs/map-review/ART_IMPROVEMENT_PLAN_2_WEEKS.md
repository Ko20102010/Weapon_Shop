# Room-by-room visual critique

## Executive summary

The current visual language is moving in the right direction: it is warmer, more coherent, and much more readable than the flat-colour base. The strongest improvement is the clarity of room identity. The biggest weakness is that several systems still feel like a mix of functional prototype art and placeholder game UI rather than a fully unified world.

The strongest current direction is not “detail-heavy realism,” but “readable, mobile-first pixel art with strong silhouettes.” That is the right priority. The challenge now is to make the world feel intentional at a glance, especially on a small phone screen.

## Global strengths

- Stronger palette than the earlier flat-colour world
- More cohesive warm woodland workshop mood
- Clearer wall depth and shadow language
- Better separation between gameplay objects and soft background decoration
- Improved room identity through local props and materials
- Better respect for route readability and collision constraints

## Global weaknesses

- Some furniture still looks generic or blocky
- Some props read as symbolic placeholders rather than intentional silhouettes
- A few room elements still depend on text labels for legibility
- In some places, contrast is not yet tuned for mobile viewing distance
- Variation is inconsistent: some rooms feel fully designed, others still feel like a layout with props added on top

## Exterior and world context

### Strengths
- The overall world mood is closer to a living workshop village than a generic dungeon floor.
- Timber, stone, brass, and foliage tones create a coherent visual family.
- The use of door thresholds, bridge detail, and forest gate silhouettes helps the world feel built.

### Weaknesses
- The exterior still risks feeling like an abstract field with props placed on it rather than a constructed terrain.
- Forest gate and bridge shapes are much better, but they need stronger silhouette separation from the surrounding ground.
- Background details should stay atmospheric, not compete with the core travel and interaction space.

### Improvement direction
- Push the bridge and gate into more distinct silhouettes.
- Use more controlled ground variation, with a little visual noise but not too much.
- Keep shrubs, stones, and roots away from navigable paths.

## Material Yard

### Strengths
- It has the potential to feel like a real work area rather than a generic storage room.
- Log piles, baskets, planters, and canopy ideas create a clear story.
- The room can read as labor and gathering with a small amount of visual clutter.

### Weaknesses
- Some props may still feel too generic or too icon-like if they are not authored with strong silhouette contrast.
- This room needs a clearer “workbench + materials + staging” rhythm.
- Empty-node states should be visually legible, or the player will lose context during low-activity moments.

### Improvement direction
- Prioritize the strongest silhouettes: stacked logs, baskets, stumps, and crate-like storage.
- Make the room read as a material flow area instead of just a set of objects.
- Keep all props readable at a glance from normal mobile viewing distance.

## Staff House

### Strengths
- The concept of individual beds, lockers, notice boards, and hiring desk is strong.
- The room can read as domestic, personal, and active if the furniture is well carved.

### Weaknesses
- If all furniture is too rectangular or too regular, the room can feel flat.
- Bed silhouettes and locker forms need stronger personality to stand out.
- This room could become visually noisy if props are too dense.

### Improvement direction
- Increase identity difference between each furniture type.
- Use consistent proportions and readable top silhouettes for beds and lockers.
- Reduce over-clutter; let a few clear props convey the room story.

## Training Yard

### Strengths
- The training yard is conceptually excellent: dummies, rack, target boards, and wear circle create a strong story.
- It has potential to become one of the clearest rooms in the map.

### Weaknesses
- If props are too small or too flat, the room may not feel active enough.
- The target board and weapon rack need extra silhouette clarity.
- The dirt wear patch should visually anchor the area without becoming a strong road-like feature.

### Improvement direction
- Make the training area feel active and directional.
- Use one or two obligatory anchors: dummy, rack, and target board.
- Keep the floor marking subtle and non-blocking.

## Forge

### Strengths
- Forge is one of the most promising rooms visually.
- Furnace, anvil, trough, and output flow are strong concepts.
- A heat-and-workflow room is a natural fit for strong color contrast and animated effects.

### Weaknesses
- The forge can easily become visually busy or too symbolic if it is not carefully simplified.
- It needs a strong “furnace-first” silhouette so it reads instantly.
- The interaction zone and output flow must not compete with the main forge core.

### Improvement direction
- Make the furnace the dominant anchor object.
- Keep the anvil and tool wall secondary but crisp.
- Use warm ember glow carefully to support readability rather than overpowering the room.
- Preserve process-flow readability: furnace → anvil → output.

## Alchemy

### Strengths
- Alchemy is visually rich and satifies the fantasy workshop mood well.
- The cauldron, bottle shelves, and herb drying rack are strong story anchors.

### Weaknesses
- It risks losing clarity if the bottle shelves, cauldron, and process pad all compete visually.
- The room needs a clear hierarchy: cauldron as primary, shelf structure as secondary, output as tertiary.
- The bottle shelf may read as decor unless it is strongly framed.

### Improvement direction
- Keep the cauldron as the visual anchor and the most saturated object in the room.
- The shelves should read as a fixed structure, not as loose junk props.
- The output flow must feel light and clean, not a cluttered table.

## Contract Warehouse

### Strengths
- The warehouse concept is clear and useful.
- Crates, loading bay, manifest board, and open handling floor are solid ideas.

### Weaknesses
- This room can become visually repetitive if crate stacks are not varied enough.
- It can also slide toward “storage generic” if it lacks a strong anchor or flow direction.

### Improvement direction
- Use a few large, readable cargo forms rather than many tiny blocks.
- Introduce one or two strong storage silhouettes and a visible loading focus.
- Preserve an open central path so it feels like a working logistics room rather than a pile of boxes.

## Retail departments

### Strengths
- Retail is one of the strongest room-identity concepts because the category distinction is visual, not just text.
- The use of rugs, banners, and shelf accents gives each category a recognizable pattern.

### Weaknesses
- Category colour can become too broad and flood the room if not carefully contained.
- It is easy to overdo the accent colours and create a room that feels decorative but not legible.
- Shelf content may become too crowded if the category markers and product forms all compete.

### Improvement direction
- Keep the colour accent localized to the shelf zone and the rug area.
- Use strong category silhouettes that can be read from a distance.
- Ensure each department reads as a distinct shelf identity without turning the whole room into a colour block.

## Customer Hall

### Strengths
- The customer hall is high-impact and likely one of the more marketable rooms.
- The checkout counter, queue studs, coin tray, and hanging sign create a strong identity.

### Weaknesses
- It is easy for this room to flatten into a generic counter and a few props.
- The checkout counter must read as a major object and not vanish into the room floor.
- The visual hierarchy needs to support both customer traffic and interaction points.

### Improvement direction
- Make the counter a primary object with a clear frame, top trim, and functional depth.
- Keep queue studs subtle but visible.
- Use the hanging sign and counter trim to create a readable story without adding clutter.

## Rare Storage

### Strengths
- Rare Storage is a good place to use a more premium, restrained, and atmospheric palette.
- Locked glowing cases can create a strong contrast against the rest of the map.

### Weaknesses
- The room can feel too dark or too flat if the light accents are not managed.
- Rare-item highlight should feel premium, not cluttered or over-lit.

### Improvement direction
- Keep the room visually quiet and controlled.
- Use violet accents sparingly to emphasize the rare premium tier.
- Allow the item glow to help player recognition without overlighting the entire room.

## Management

### Strengths
- This room can be highly readable with desk, papers, blueprints, lamp, and seal details.
- It has a natural “planning and oversight” identity.

### Weaknesses
- It might be too flat unless the desk and table forms have strong silhouette differentiation.
- The station plaques need to read clearly from a distance if they are intended to support navigation.

### Improvement direction
- Create a clean desk silhouette with a document stack and a bold table anchor.
- Keep plaque detail readable and distinct, but not too decorative.
- Ensure the room still feels clean and important, not overstuffed.

## Overall assessment

The current art direction is strong enough to maintain. It is not a random or inconsistent visual treatment. The real challenge is not “what style,” but “how much clarity and how much discipline.”

The visual language works best when:
- silhouettes lead the design
- local materials define the room
- background remains secondary
- labels are a backup, not a requirement

The art is not yet at “final polished game identity,” but it is close enough that a disciplined, device-tested polish pass can get it there quickly.

## Recommended priorities

1. Make room silhouettes read instantly on phone screens.
2. Replace placeholders with dedicated pixel props.
3. Reduce generic furniture and keep local identity strong.
4. Validate on iPhone Safari before calling the pass done.
